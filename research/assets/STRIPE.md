## Thesis and Evidence

Assumption under review: OpenAI succeeds with widely adopted AI assistants and devices.
Stripe could benefit from resulting commercial activity, but OpenAI success alone does not
prove Stripe's market share, retained fees, margins, or investment returns.

- Stripe co-developed the Agentic Commerce Protocol with OpenAI. Its February 24, 2026
  update reported 2025 payment volume of US$1.9 trillion, up 34%, and profitability.
  Payment volume is not Stripe revenue or cash flow. [Stripe update](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-2025-update)
- Stripe announced an agreement to acquire OpenRouter on August 19, 2026. Token routing
  broadens the potential AI monetization exposure; closing, consideration, dilution, and
  integration economics must be rechecked. [Stripe announcement](https://stripe.com/newsroom/news/stripe-agrees-to-acquire-openrouter)
- OpenAI shifted toward merchants' own checkout experiences on March 24, 2026, and Visa
  announced a separate OpenAI collaboration on June 10. Stripe is not an exclusive payment
  gatekeeper. [OpenAI update](https://openai.com/index/powering-product-discovery-in-chatgpt/)
  and [Visa announcement](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22496.html)

Evidence confidence is high on the documented relationships and low on executable entry value.
The metadata confidence score is a qualitative research assessment, not a return probability.

## Valuation and Access Gates

The February 24, 2026 employee tender referenced a US$159 billion valuation. It is a dated
company-level reference, not a live quote, current fair value, or an approved entry.
At unchanged share count, 2x and 3x that reference imply US$318 billion and US$477 billion;
these are arithmetic hurdles, not forecasts, and exclude fees, dilution, and currency effects.

Before considering a purchase, verify:

1. Current financials, including net revenue, margins, cash flow, and acquisition economics.
2. Fully diluted capitalization, share-class rights, and the all-in executable price.
3. Ownership chain, transfer approval, liquidity restrictions, fees, and account eligibility.
4. Incremental agentic revenue versus purchases already processed by Stripe.
5. Bear/base/bull per-share value, competition, regulatory risk, and invalidation.
6. An explicit private-investment mandate or a confirmed public listing, followed by PM approval.

The earlier idea of up to 10% of a hypothetical speculative sleeve is a discussion limit,
not an actual portfolio weight, target, reserved allocation, or approval. Current policy
capacity for this research-only sleeve is zero. No existing allocations are changed.
