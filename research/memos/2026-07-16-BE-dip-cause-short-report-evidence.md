## Evidence Summary

The July BE dislocation has a specific near-term catalyst: a July 8 short-seller report. Bloom filed an 8-K on July 9 stating that Hunterbrook Media had published a report on July 8 and disclosed that the outlet may hold positions that would profit from a decline in Bloom's stock. Bloom rejected the report's accounting and financial-reporting claims, referred investors back to its audited and SEC-filed financial statements, and specifically rejected the scandium-oxide supply-chain conclusion. Bloom stated it has enough scandium oxide to meet current fuel-cell demand and backlog, that supply is not dependent on China, and that it has visibility to support production of 25 GW of fuel cells per year.

That 8-K does not independently prove Hunterbrook is wrong. It does, however, bound the immediate cause of the July 13-16 dislocations: the selloff is not an unexplained filing gap or hidden missed earnings release; it is a market dispute around accounting/backlog/supply-chain credibility after a disclosed short report and company rebuttal.

Primary operating evidence through Q1 2026 remains constructive but not clean enough for capital approval by itself. Bloom reported Q1 2026 revenue of $751.1 million, up 130.4% year over year, product revenue of $653.3 million, up 208.4%, GAAP gross margin of 30.0%, non-GAAP gross margin of 31.5%, operating income of $72.2 million, and operating cash flow of $73.6 million. Bloom also raised full-year 2026 guidance to revenue of $3.4 billion to $3.8 billion, non-GAAP gross margin around 34%, non-GAAP operating income of $600 million to $750 million, and non-GAAP EPS of $1.85 to $2.25. Management framed the business around onsite power for the digital age.

The Q1 2026 10-Q supports the thesis but also preserves the key anti-thesis. Concentration is material: U.S. revenue was 91% of total revenue in Q1 2026, one customer accounted for about 30% of accounts receivable, and two customers accounted for about 50% and 12% of total revenue. The filing also shows substantial debt: about $2.6 billion of recourse debt outstanding at March 31, 2026, plus disclosure that future capital needs may require equity or debt financing and that failure to obtain financing on favorable terms could affect revenue, cash flow, and financial position.

Warranty and product-service risk remain visible in the financial statements. As of March 31, 2026, product performance warranties were $34.3 million and service warranties were $10.9 million, for total warranty liabilities of $45.2 million. That is not a new July break, but it is directly relevant to the local anti-thesis around fuel-cell reliability and delivery economics.

The July 6 IR release says Q2 2026 results will be released after market close on July 28, 2026. That means the current evidence set is stale for Q2 execution, and the short-report dispute will not be fully adjudicated by primary quarterly results until that release.

Macro/regime context is also adverse for BE and the power-bottleneck sleeve. The July 16 FERC primary regime event says FERC directed NERC to file computational-load reliability standards and registry criteria by December 31, 2026. That is not company-specific thesis damage, but it raises compliance/curtailment risk for large loads while potentially increasing the value of reliable onsite power. Middle East conflict events add broader risk-off pressure but do not specifically invalidate Bloom's datacenter thesis.

## Resolution

The immediate source gap is resolved: the July BE dislocation is best framed as a short-report/accounting/backlog/supply-chain credibility shock plus broad AI/power risk-off, not as a found primary-source operating miss, guidance cut, customer cancellation, or filing-disclosed failure.

The thesis is not cleared for a buy/add decision. Bloom's own filings leave unresolved PM-level questions around customer concentration, backlog recognizability, warranty and service economics, financing needs, valuation after the selloff, and whether the July 9 company rebuttal is sufficient without Q2 results.

## Remaining Uncertainty

- Whether Q2 results on July 28, 2026 confirm backlog conversion, customer quality, margins, and cash generation.
- Whether Hunterbrook's accounting/backlog/supply-chain allegations reveal any issue not rebutted by the July 9 8-K.
- Whether customer concentration and financing dependence warrant a lower position cap even if the datacenter onsite-power thesis remains intact.
- Whether FERC computational-load reliability standards are net positive for onsite power demand or net negative through added compliance and curtailment risk.

## Portfolio PM Decision Needed

Decide whether BE remains `RESEARCH_REQUIRED` until Q2 results and a claim-by-claim short-report review are complete, or whether the bounded cause is enough to move BE back to watch/add-on-dip consideration with strict sizing, customer-concentration limits, and explicit invalidation triggers tied to backlog conversion, warranty/service margins, and financing access.
