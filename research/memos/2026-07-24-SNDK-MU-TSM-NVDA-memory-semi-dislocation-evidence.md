## Findings

### The July dislocations remain broad AI-semiconductor risk repricing, not an identified primary-source operating break

The common factor across MU, SNDK, TSM, and NVDA is not a disclosed cut in AI demand. Local regime events show confirmed Middle East energy/shipping escalation into July 23, higher oil/volatility, weaker SPY/QQQ, and broad AI/power/miner selloffs. That is enough to explain a crowded AI-beta and long-duration earnings drawdown without treating price action as thesis evidence.

TSMC's July 16 Q2 results directly argue against a current upstream AI compute demand break. Q2 revenue was $40.20 billion, above the midpoint of prior guidance, with 67.7% gross margin and 60.3% operating margin. Q3 guidance was $44.6-$45.8 billion of revenue, 65.0%-67.0% gross margin, and 56.0%-58.0% operating margin. This supports ongoing leading-edge and advanced-packaging demand, while leaving valuation, Taiwan/geopolitical, customer concentration, and capex execution as PM sizing risks.

NVIDIA's July 21 Vera Rubin update also supports the AI infrastructure demand side. NVIDIA said Vera Rubin NVL72 production is ramping with racks running at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, backed by 350-plus factory sites in 30 countries. It also described first deployments and benchmarks at CoreWeave and Google Cloud. This does not make NVDA buyable at any price, but it is inconsistent with a fresh primary-source accelerator demand collapse.

### SNDK has the largest unresolved repeat signal, but no fresh primary source shows a July thesis break

SNDK remains the most fragile name in this cluster because the decision file is already `RESEARCH_REQUIRED`, the price moves are the largest, and the thesis depends on normalized NAND margins rather than only current reported results.

The latest Sandisk IR index still shows no newer operating release after July 9. The visible sequence is July 9 FY Q4/FY2026 results call plus August 13 Investor Day notice, July 2 BiCS10 sampling, and April 30 Q3 results. The Q3 release showed revenue of $5.95 billion, up 97% sequentially and 251% year over year, Datacenter revenue of $1.467 billion, and Q4 guidance for $7.75-$8.25 billion revenue with non-GAAP EPS of $30-$33. The July 2 BiCS10 release supports the technology roadmap, citing a 1Tb TLC sampling milestone, 332 layers, up to 4.8Gb/s interface speed, and 59% bit-density improvement versus BiCS8.

The same Sandisk materials also explain why the equity can keep selling off without a discrete break: NAND demand volatility, ASP changes, competitor pricing, long-term-agreement execution/financial/market exposure, Kioxia reliance, trade policy, supply disruption, and customer concentration are all live risk factors. The source gap is resolved as "no new primary-source break found"; the valuation and normalized-margin question is not resolved.

### MU evidence remains constructive on HBM demand but still exposed to peak-cycle normalization

The July 16 MU memo already resolved the July 15-16 gap as valuation, crowding, capacity-response, and normalized-margin risk rather than HBM execution failure. The current pass found no newer primary Micron source changing that conclusion. The June 22 Micron-Anthropic agreement remains thesis-supportive: it covers memory/storage AI architecture work, supply across Micron's data-center portfolio, enterprise Claude adoption, and a strategic investment. Micron's HBM/DRAM/SSD exposure therefore remains relevant to frontier AI infrastructure.

The unresolved issue is still whether Q4's guided gross-margin base is peak-cycle and how much of the current earnings power is protected by strategic customer agreements if spot memory prices normalize. That is a Portfolio PM valuation and sizing decision, not a remaining unknown-source gap.
