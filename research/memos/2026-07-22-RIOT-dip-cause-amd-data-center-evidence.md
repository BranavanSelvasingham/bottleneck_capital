# RIOT Dip Cause and Data-Center Evidence

## Resolver conclusion

RIOT's July 2026 dislocations are bounded as miner-to-datacenter execution risk, bitcoin-beta/crowded-AI repricing, financing/capex risk, power/regulatory risk, and macro/geopolitical risk. I did not find primary-source evidence of an AMD lease cancellation, guidance withdrawal, liquidity default, exchange notice, site loss, regulatory block, or Situational Awareness exit/reduction. The new primary evidence strengthens the "power/site optionality" side of the thesis, but does not by itself authorize a portfolio decision.

## What appears resolved

- The source gap is no longer "AI/data-center conversion is purely speculative." Riot has named tenant evidence through AMD, contracted capacity that doubled from 25 MW to 50 MW, and reported data-center revenue.
- The July price dislocation does not map to a found primary-source cancellation, default, AMD relationship break, site loss, or official SA exit/reduction.
- The most likely cause is repricing of a still-early conversion story: bitcoin-linked revenue, capex and retrofit burden, remaining unleased capacity, customer concentration, and broad AI/miner risk-off under elevated power/rates/geopolitical stress.

## Remaining uncertainty

- AMD delivery status after May 2026 needs the next company update; the Q1 evidence showed delivery in progress, not full steady-state proof.
- Only 50 MW is contracted against a much larger stated power portfolio; the remaining conversion runway still requires customers, financing, construction, interconnection, and power-cost proof.
- AMD concentration matters: the data-center thesis is now real but still narrow.
- The Terrestrial Energy MOU is not a binding nuclear deployment, regulatory approval, financing package, or near-term power hedge.
- Bitcoin mining still dominates Q1 revenue, and Riot sold bitcoin in Q1 to fund operations and strategic moves; mining economics remain material to valuation and downside.
