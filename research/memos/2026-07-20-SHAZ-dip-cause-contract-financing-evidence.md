## Finding

The source gap is bounded, not fully de-risked.

No primary-source evidence found in the reviewed issuer/SEC materials shows a fresh July operating failure, customer cancellation, guidance cut, liquidity default, SEC-disclosed fraud/governance failure, data-center demand collapse, or SA exit behind the July 13-20 SHAZ dislocations.

The strongest positive evidence is customer-contract and financing validation: the July 16 five-year US$1.32 billion service agreement increases contracted capacity from the June 18 disclosure, and the June 29 financing close materially improves funding visibility for a capital-intensive AI-factory buildout.

The dislocation is better bounded as a valuation, dilution, convert, execution-timing, customer-verification, and macro/crowded-AI-beta reset:

- The June financing was large and constructive for runway, but it also introduced substantial new common/pre-funded warrant issuance and US$700 million of convertible debt.
- The July 16 contract is forward-looking, with revenue expected to begin in Q1/Q2 2027 rather than current-quarter cash conversion.
- Q1 2026 SEC evidence still describes cash-consuming expansion, material capex deposits, future funding needs, colocation commitments, and early-stage operating scale.
- SHAZ remains an emerging, small-cap neocloud where the public evidence verifies momentum but not yet durable unit economics, customer identity/credit quality, deployment execution, power availability, or conversion of contracted capacity into free cash flow.
- The latest local macro/regime backdrop remains adverse for long-duration AI infrastructure: Middle East conflict/shipping risk, elevated rates/financing risk, and FERC computational-load reliability work raise the discount-rate, power, and execution-risk burden without independently breaking the SHAZ company thesis.

## Remaining uncertainty

- Customer identity and credit quality for the July 16 global AI lab agreement are not disclosed in the company page reviewed.
- Contract economics, gross margin, cash conversion timing, penalties, minimum commitments, power cost pass-through, and cancellation/termination rights remain unresolved.
- The June financing reduces immediate liquidity risk but raises dilution and convert-overhang questions.
- Deployment of 62,000-plus NVIDIA GPUs by mid-2027 remains a large execution and supply-chain task.
- Governance and related-party risk remain relevant because the Q1 filing disclosed related-party convertible-note participation and earlier related-party share issuance, although the filing states the December notes were on the same terms as unrelated third-party investors.
