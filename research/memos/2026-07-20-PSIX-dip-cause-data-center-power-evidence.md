## Source gap resolved

Active signal events flagged PSIX price dislocations on July 13-17, including the July 17 close at $30.25 after a 4.8% one-day decline and 4.6% gap-down signal. The research question was whether primary evidence showed a fresh company-specific thesis break in data-center power demand, backlog/order conversion, liquidity, emissions/regulatory exposure, supply chain, or filings.

## Evidence

- No new July company filing, guidance cut, customer cancellation, debt default, exchange notice, or operating update was found after the June 9 proxy filings. The only July 13 primary company release located was the scheduling notice for Q2 results on August 6, 2026.
- Q1 2026 was weaker year over year, but the weakness was already disclosed before the active July dip triggers. Net sales were $128.6 million, down 5% year over year; gross profit was $29.4 million, down 27%; net income was $7.3 million, down 62%; adjusted EBITDA was $13.9 million, down 47%.
- Power Systems remained the core business at $96.5 million, 75% of Q1 revenue. The disclosed Q1 pressure was lower Power Systems sales from oil-and-gas softness, uneven order patterns, and shipment timing for data-center-related products.
- The data-center thesis is not invalidated by primary sources, but it remains unproven. Management said data-center demand remained solid and expected stronger second-half sales as larger Power Systems orders moved into production, while explicitly warning that shipment timing, customer scheduling, manufacturing throughput, supply chain, and related variables could prevent a uniformly stronger second half.
- The Wisconsin ramp remains the central operating risk. Q1 margin compression reflected elevated production costs tied to capacity ramp-up for data-center-related applications; management expected those costs to continue.
- Liquidity did not show an acute break in the Q1 filing. PSI reported $45.1 million cash and cash equivalents, $103.4 million total debt, $95.0 million drawn under the revolver, and stated that cash flow, available funds, and financing access should be sufficient for at least the next twelve months and beyond.
- Legal risk is material but already disclosed. The Q1 10-Q disclosed a securities class action alleging misstatements around demand, manufacturing capacity, and operating inefficiencies for data-center product lines; PSI said it believed the claims were without merit, the matter was early stage, and no loss range could be reasonably estimated.
- Macro/regime context worsened into the same period: Middle East shipping/energy conflict, elevated rates/inflation risk, and FERC computational-load reliability-standard work increase discount-rate, energy, grid-regulation, and execution-risk pressure for power-bottleneck names. That context supports a broad risk/valuation reset explanation rather than proving a PSIX-specific thesis break.

## Conclusion

The active PSIX dip source gap is bounded: primary evidence does not show a fresh July company-specific operating failure, customer cancellation, guidance cut, filing-disclosed liquidity break, regulatory failure, or data-center demand collapse. The dislocation is better explained as repricing of already-disclosed Q1 margin/order-timing weakness, litigation overhang tied directly to the data-center ramp narrative, valuation/crowding pressure, oil-and-gas softness, and elevated macro/rates/grid-regulation risk.

This does not clear PSIX for capital. The unresolved issue is not "what caused the July dip?" but whether Q2 results confirm that larger Power Systems/data-center orders are converting into revenue with improving throughput and acceptable gross margin.
