## Evidence

No newer primary-source operating release was found after the July 9 8-K. Bloom's public newsroom still shows the July 6 Q2 reporting-date notice as the latest item, and the investor press-release index still lists the July 6 Q2 notice, June 30 Brookfield partnership expansion, June 15 data-center power report, April 28 Q1 results, and April 13 Oracle partnership as the latest operating/IR releases.

The July 9 8-K remains the key company response to the immediate credibility shock. Bloom disclosed that Hunterbrook Media had published a report on July 8 and may hold positions that would profit from a stock-price decline. Bloom rejected the report's accounting and financial-reporting claims, referred investors to SEC-filed statements, rejected the scandium-oxide conclusion, said current demand and backlog are covered, and said its supply chain visibility supports production of 25 GW of fuel cells per year. That filing bounds the known company-specific catalyst, but it does not independently adjudicate every short-report claim.

The latest pre-dislocation demand evidence remains constructive. On April 13, Bloom announced an expanded Oracle relationship under a master services agreement supporting up to 2.8 GW, with 1.2 GW initially contracted and deployment underway. On June 30, Bloom and Brookfield announced that Brookfield expanded its AI infrastructure power-project financing framework from $5 billion to $25 billion. These releases support the AI onsite-power thesis and explain why valuation and expectations became high, but they do not remove customer concentration, financing, contract-quality, execution, or margin risk.

The latest financial evidence is still Q1, not Q2. Bloom reported record Q1 2026 results and raised FY2026 guidance in April. The prior July 16 memo already captured the main unresolved anti-thesis from the Q1 filing: customer concentration, material debt, future financing needs, and warranty/service economics. None of those risks is new on July 24, but the market is repricing them more severely before the July 28 Q2 report.

The July 24 local regime backdrop supports a broad risk-off explanation. Active macro/geopolitical events include Middle East energy/shipping escalation, elevated rates/inflation risk, U.S. grid/regulatory pressure around computational load, and a global trade/tariff shock. For BE specifically, these channels raise discount-rate, financing, input-cost, supply-chain, power-site, customer-capex, and valuation risk. They do not prove a Bloom-specific customer cancellation, guidance cut, liquidity default, exchange notice, or product failure.

## Resolution

The July 24 BE source gap is bounded as a repeat short-report/accounting/backlog/scandium-supply credibility and valuation reset, amplified by broad AI/power infrastructure risk-off and macro/regime pressure before Q2 results. I did not find a newer primary-source Bloom operating miss, guidance withdrawal, customer cancellation, financing default, liquidity warning, exchange notice, product reliability disclosure, or data-center demand break after the July 16 memo.

This does not clear BE for capital. The evidence gap that matters for Portfolio PM is now Q2 execution and claim-by-claim credibility, not whether there is a hidden July 24 primary release. The July 28 Q2 results remain the next required primary evidence gate.
