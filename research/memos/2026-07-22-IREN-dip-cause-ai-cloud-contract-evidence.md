## Evidence

### 1. The July 20 primary-source update strengthens the AI-cloud conversion case.

IREN announced $2.8 billion of new multi-year cloud-services contracts with leading AI developers and raised its year-end 2026 AI Cloud ARR target from $3.7 billion to more than $4 billion. It said about 85% of that target is now under contract, with customers including Microsoft, NVIDIA, Perplexity, Figure AI, Together AI, Fluidstack, Fireworks AI, Fal AI, Hume AI, and one additional leading AI developer.

This is direct primary evidence that IREN is no longer merely a Bitcoin miner with vague AI optionality. The customer list, contract value, and ARR under contract support the miner-to-datacenter thesis that power-secured sites can convert into contracted AI-cloud infrastructure.

### 2. Funding risk improved, but did not disappear.

The July 20 release says recent contracts include customer prepayments equal to roughly 45% of associated GPU capex and that IREN held about $7.6 billion of cash and equivalents at June 30, 2026, including $1.7 billion of restricted cash connected with Microsoft GPU financing. This reduces the immediate concern that the AI-cloud ramp is unsupported by capital.

The memo should not overstate this as a solved balance sheet. IREN still has to deliver, commission, test, and achieve customer acceptance for the GPUs; ARR is not GAAP revenue; future contracts may not carry the same prepayment terms; and the May Q3 update still showed a net loss, lower sequential revenue, impairments tied to decommissioned mining hardware, and ongoing financing needs for GPU/data-center expansion.

### 3. The pre-July 20 selloff has an identifiable non-operating cause: governance/dilution backlash plus crowding/rate/geopolitical pressure.

IREN filed an 8-K on July 1 disclosing 9,099,328 RSUs granted to each co-CEO, vesting annually over four years with a two-year post-vesting holding period, and no further equity incentive grant until fiscal 2031. The July 8 shareholder letter from the independent chair defended the award as founder-retention/alignment compensation after a large increase in market capitalization and customer wins.

That filing gives a concrete primary-source explanation for governance and dilution concern during the July window. It does not prove an operating break. It does, however, create a real PM issue: even if AI-cloud execution is improving, the governance package raises dilution, capital-allocation, and alignment questions for a highly capital-intensive company.

The broader July context also matters. Local regime events show Middle East escalation, elevated inflation/rates risk, FERC large-load reliability scrutiny, and widespread AI/miner/semicap dislocations. Those are consistent with valuation/crowding and financing-risk repricing across long-duration AI infrastructure names rather than an IREN-specific customer cancellation.

### 4. No primary-source thesis break found.

I did not find primary-source evidence through the sources above of:

- Customer cancellation or default.
- NVIDIA or Microsoft contract failure.
- Guidance cut in the July 20 update.
- Liquidity default, covenant breach, exchange notice, or solvency warning.
- Data-center power/site loss.
- GPU delivery failure already disclosed as a material event.
- A filing-confirmed Situational Awareness exit or reduction.

The opposite is true on customer proof: IREN reported more contracted AI-cloud demand after the dip events.

## Interpretation

The July IREN source gap is bounded as governance/dilution backlash, valuation/crowding reset, macro/rates/geopolitical pressure, and execution-risk repricing around a fast AI-cloud buildout. It is not, based on the checked primary sources, a confirmed customer-demand break, power-site break, financing default, or failure of the miner-to-datacenter conversion thesis.

The evidence is stronger than the existing baseline in one important way: primary IREN disclosures now show a broader AI-cloud customer base, higher 2026 ARR target, meaningful contracted coverage, and customer prepayments. That supports moving IREN to the Portfolio PM for a decision refresh. It does not itself authorize a trade.

## Remaining Uncertainty

- Commissioning, testing, and customer acceptance timing for the 480 MW 2026 AI-cloud capacity target.
- How much of ARR converts into GAAP revenue, cash flow, and durable margins.
- Actual GPU financing terms, restricted-cash use, and any additional dilution.
- Whether the governance backlash affects cost of capital, shareholder trust, or future compensation discipline.
- Customer concentration and counterparty credit risk, even with a broader customer list.
- Power, permitting, grid-load, curtailment, and regulatory execution risk, especially under the July FERC computational-load reliability-standard process.
- Valuation after the rebound from July 20 contract news.
