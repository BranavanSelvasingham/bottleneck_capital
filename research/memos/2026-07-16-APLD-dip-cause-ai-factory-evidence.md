# APLD July 2026 Dislocation Evidence Memo

Date: 2026-07-16
Scope: Research resolver memo only; no portfolio decision.
Ticker: APLD
Decision owner: Portfolio PM

## Question

Resolve whether the July 13-16 APLD price dislocations indicate a primary-source break in
the Applied Digital AI/HPC datacenter thesis, especially contract quality, financing,
delivery risk, power/site economics, or customer demand.

## Evidence Reviewed

- Applied Digital investor relations press-release index checked 2026-07-16. The latest
  company release before the July 16 close was a July 15, 2026 notice that fiscal Q4 and
  FY2026 results would be released after market close on July 27, 2026, with no disclosed
  preannouncement, guidance cut, customer cancellation, construction failure, or financing
  failure in that notice.
- Applied Digital July 1, 2026 release stated that Phase 1 of Building 2 at Polaris Forge 1
  reached ready-for-service on schedule, delivering 75 MW of operational AI capacity and
  bringing total live campus capacity to 175 MW. The same release framed Polaris Forge 1 as
  fully leased and contracted for 400 MW of critical IT load at full buildout.
- Applied Digital June 26, 2026 Form 8-K disclosed a material credit agreement amendment:
  secured revolving commitments increased to $430.0 million, with ability to add up to
  another $120.0 million for total potential revolver capacity of $550.0 million. The same
  8-K disclosed a sixth amendment to the preferred equity purchase agreement increasing
  Series G preferred stock commitment capacity from $1.59 billion to $2.0 billion.
- Applied Digital June 9, 2026 release priced $1.59 billion of 7.000% senior secured notes
  due 2031 at par through APLD ComputeCo 3, intended to fund 150 MW of critical IT load at
  ELN-04, repay the bridge loan, fund debt service reserves, and pay transaction expenses.
- Applied Digital June 8, 2026 release announced a 210 MW, 15-year take-or-pay lease at
  Delta Forge 2 with a U.S.-based high investment-grade hyperscaler. The release stated the
  agreement represented approximately $5.2 billion of base-term contracted revenue, brought
  the contracted portfolio to 1.4 GW of critical IT load, and left approximately 70% of
  contracted revenue backed by U.S.-based investment-grade hyperscalers.
- Applied Digital May 20, 2026 release announced a 300 MW, 15-year take-or-pay Polaris Forge
  3 lease with the same U.S.-based high investment-grade hyperscaler previously signed at
  Delta Forge 1, representing approximately $7.5 billion of base-term contracted value and
  pushing contracted capacity across four AI Factory campuses to 1.2 GW.
- Applied Digital fiscal Q3 2026 release reported revenue of $126.6 million, adjusted EBITDA
  of $44.1 million, and stated that the first 100 MW Polaris Forge 1 building was fully
  operational. Management also said North Dakota construction remained on schedule and
  described CoreWeave lease-credit enhancements through an investment-grade SPV structure,
  springing guarantees, and a $50 million letter of credit for the ELN-02 lease.
- Local market events show a July 16 APLD close at roughly $26.44 versus $29.03 prior close,
  a one-day drop of about 8.9%. Investing.com historical data independently shows a similar
  July 16 close around $26.28-$26.43 after a June high near the mid-to-high $40s. This is
  relevant as a valuation/crowding reset, not as a technical signal.

## Finding

The source gap behind the July 13-16 APLD dislocation is bounded. I found no primary-source
evidence of an acute company-specific break in customer demand, lease validity, delivery
execution, or funding access during the reviewed period. The primary-source tape before the
selloff is actually constructive on demand and execution: new/expanded hyperscaler leases,
more than 1 GW contracted capacity, on-schedule delivery of another Polaris Forge phase, and
incremental secured/project-style financing.

The dislocation is better framed as a valuation, financing-complexity, dilution/PEPA,
leverage, execution-timing, customer-concentration, and broad AI-infrastructure crowding
reset after a very large rerating. The risks are still real: funding is capital-intensive,
PEPA capacity creates dilution overhang, project execution has long lead times, lease revenue
is back-end and counterparty-dependent, and the July 27 FY2026 results remain an important
near-term validation point. But those are known underwriting risks rather than newly found
thesis damage.

## Remaining Uncertainty

- Need FY2026 results and management commentary on July 27, 2026 for updated cash, capex,
  debt, PEPA usage, construction timing, and campus-level funding needs.
- Need Portfolio PM valuation work after the drawdown: enterprise value versus contracted
  base-term revenue, debt/project-finance obligations, preferred-equity dilution, and
  time-to-cash-flow.
- Need continued monitoring of hyperscaler credit support, CoreWeave-related structures,
  customer concentration, utility interconnection/regulatory exposure, and whether FERC/NERC
  computational-load reliability work changes project timelines or curtailment risk.
