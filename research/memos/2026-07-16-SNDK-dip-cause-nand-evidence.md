## Findings

### Operating evidence still supports the NAND/storage thesis, but valuation risk is extreme

The Q3 FY26 deck showed a very strong recent operating base:

- Revenue was $5.95 billion, up 97% quarter over quarter and 251% year over year.
- Non-GAAP gross margin was 78.4%, up 27.3 percentage points quarter over quarter.
- Datacenter revenue was $1.467 billion, up 233% quarter over quarter.
- Management guided Q4 FY26 revenue to $7.75-$8.25 billion, with non-GAAP gross margin of 79%-81% and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $30-$33.
- Cash and equivalents were $3.74 billion, adjusted free cash flow was $2.955 billion, and the company said it had repaid debt and announced a $6 billion share repurchase program.

This does not show immediate thesis damage in the primary materials. It does, however, show why the equity is highly sensitive to any belief that NAND margins, supply discipline, or contract durability could normalize.

### Customer-contract evidence reduces but does not remove cyclicality risk

The Q3 deck says Sandisk secured three New Business Model partnerships backed by firm financial guarantees, with two more added in fiscal Q4. That supports the "AI storage scarcity with better visibility" thesis, but it also creates a new risk class: execution, financial, and market exposure from long-term agreements. Sandisk's July 2 BiCS10 release explicitly lists long-term-agreement exposure, demand volatility, average selling price fluctuations, competitor pricing, customer relationship changes, and Kioxia reliance as risks.

The practical reading: the selloff does not appear to be caused by a disclosed customer cancellation, demand break, or balance-sheet shock. The unresolved issue is whether the market is repricing normalized memory-cycle earnings versus the unusually high current margin base.

### Technology roadmap remains intact

The July 2 BiCS10 release says Sandisk is sampling 10th-generation 3D NAND with:

- 1Tb TLC density greater than 29Gb/mm2.
- 59% bit-density improvement versus BiCS8.
- Up to 4.8Gb/s NAND interface speed, 33% above BiCS8.
- 332 memory layers and support for Toggle DDR6.0, SCA protocol, and PI-LTT.

This is positive evidence for the roadmap and AI/data-intensive workload relevance. It is not enough by itself to approve capital, because sampling is not the same as high-volume revenue, margin durability, or customer concentration safety.

### No newer operating filing was found before the scheduled August catalysts

Sandisk IR says the FY Q4/FY2026 earnings call is scheduled for August 5, 2026 and Investor Day for August 13, 2026. The SEC filings index showed July/June insider forms and a May 22 SD filing on page 1; the July 2 BiCS10 release points investors to the latest Annual Report on Form 10-K filed August 21, 2025 and Quarterly Report on Form 10-Q filed May 1, 2026.

I did not find a newer primary operating filing or guidance update dated after the Q3 deck that would explain the July 13-16 selloff as company-specific thesis damage.

## Bounded Cause Assessment

The SNDK dislocation is best bounded as:

1. A memory/NAND basket drawdown after a very large YTD rerating.
2. Repricing of cycle-normalized margins and supply response risk, not a disclosed demand break.
3. Concern that extraordinary Q3/Q4 gross margins and NBM economics may not be durable through a full NAND cycle.
4. Competitive/supply risk from memory peers and potential future capacity, which is consistent with the anti-thesis already in the asset file.

The evidence does not resolve valuation attractiveness. It only resolves the immediate source gap: I found no primary evidence of an acute company-specific break in NAND/storage demand, customer agreements, balance sheet, or technology roadmap as of the sources checked.

## Remaining Uncertainty

- The August 5 FY Q4/FY2026 results and August 13 Investor Day are the next primary catalysts.
- The core unresolved question is valuation under normalized NAND margins versus the current supernormal margin and contract base.
- Long-term agreement economics require scrutiny: guarantees improve visibility but may add execution and pricing flexibility risk.
- BiCS10 is a roadmap positive, but commercialization timing and margin contribution remain unproven.
- Competitive supply response and customer concentration remain the main anti-thesis areas.
