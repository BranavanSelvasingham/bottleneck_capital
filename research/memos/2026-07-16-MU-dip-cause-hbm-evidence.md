## Findings

The July 15-16 MU drawdown appears to be a repricing of peak-memory-cycle and capacity-expansion risk, not an identified company-specific break in Micron's HBM execution or AI-memory demand evidence.

Micron's latest primary results remain strong. The company reported Q3 FY2026 revenue of $41.456 billion, non-GAAP EPS of $25.11, operating cash flow of $25.39 billion, and guided Q4 revenue to $50.0 billion plus or minus $1.0 billion, gross margin around 86%, and non-GAAP EPS of $31.00 plus or minus $1.00. Product evidence remains intact: HBM4 was in high-volume shipments for the lead customer platform, HBM4E development was underway for expected calendar-2027 volume production, G9 PCIe Gen6 high-performance SSD was in volume production, and 245TB QLC SSD shipments had commenced.

The 10-Q supports the demand side of the thesis but also explains why the stock can sell off without a current thesis break. Micron states AI-driven memory and storage demand is outpacing industry supply, lifting pricing and margins. It also states strategic customer agreements include take-or-pay multi-year volume commitments, with many fixed or banded pricing structures and floors that management says should remain above prior peak-cycle margins. That directly supports durability versus an ordinary spot-memory cycle.

The unresolved risk is supply response and normalization, not current demand failure. The same 10-Q warns that Micron and competitors plan new fab capacity and that worldwide supply growth not matched by demand could pressure average selling prices. This is the right anti-thesis for the July 15-16 selloff: investors may be discounting future oversupply and margin normalization after Micron announced larger U.S. investment commitments and supply-chain financing.

The July 9 primary releases reinforce both sides. Positive: Micron raised planned U.S. investment to more than $250 billion through 2035, tied the spending to AI-era memory demand, reported New York fab construction progress, and said Idaho output is expected in mid-calendar 2027 and late-calendar 2028. It also announced up to $3 billion for U.S. semiconductor ecosystem investments, including $500 million strategic financing for GlobalWafers and a 10-year 300mm silicon wafer supply agreement. Risk: these same capacity and supply-assurance actions increase the market's focus on whether today's very high margins are peak-cycle rather than normalized.

The June 22 Micron-Anthropic agreement is directly thesis-relevant. It includes memory and storage AI architecture collaboration, a supply agreement across Micron's data-center portfolio, enterprise Claude adoption at Micron, and a strategic investment in Anthropic. That evidence argues against an immediate customer-demand or HBM relevance break.
