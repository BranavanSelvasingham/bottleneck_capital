## Findings

CLSK's July dislocations are now better bounded as execution-financing and crowded miner-to-datacenter repricing, not a primary-source proof of thesis break.

The material new evidence is the July 14 SEC-filed Exhibit 99.1. CleanSpark disclosed a 20-year triple-net infrastructure lease at Sandersville with a high-investment-grade global technology company, 175 MW of critical IT load, expected first deliveries in Q4 2027, approximately $6.6 billion of expected initial-term contract value, and up to $11.6 billion if both five-year extension options are exercised. The same filing says the tenant also signed a letter of intent and exclusivity arrangement over CleanSpark's Texas portfolio of 718 acres and up to 885 MW of secured and planned power capacity.

This directly addresses the prior local baseline gap that CleanSpark's AI/data-center economics were not primary-source visible. The tenant remains confidential, and the Texas arrangement is still an LOI/exclusivity arrangement rather than a definitive lease. The Sandersville project also requires large execution and financing follow-through: the SEC exhibit cites estimated landlord project costs of $10 million to $12 million per MW of critical IT load and explicitly lists risks around financing, construction milestones, potential rent abatements or termination, covenants, substantial additional capital needs, indebtedness, third-party development performance, equipment availability, regulatory approvals, electrical power availability, ongoing power supply, and changes to AI/HPC infrastructure needs.

The July 7 operational update still shows CleanSpark as a bitcoin-mining-driven operator today: June bitcoin production was 614 BTC; operational hashrate was 50 EH/s; average operating hashrate was 42.6 EH/s; deployed fleet was 225,137 miners; power under contract was 1.8 GW; utilized power was 808 MW; and bitcoin holdings were 13,924 at June 30. That supports the view that the equity remains exposed to bitcoin/mining beta while the data-center lease backlog is converted into delivered capacity.

No primary-source evidence was found of customer cancellation, guidance withdrawal, liquidity default, exchange notice, site loss, power-loss event, covenant breach, or official Situational Awareness exit/reduction after the latest available Q1 2026 13F. The latest local macro/regime context remains adverse for financing and construction risk: Middle East energy/shipping escalation, elevated rates/inflation uncertainty, and grid-reliability/regulatory pressure all increase execution risk for capital-intensive AI infrastructure without independently breaking the CLSK company thesis.

## Remaining Uncertainty

- Tenant identity is undisclosed, limiting independent credit and strategic-dependency assessment.
- The Texas portfolio is under LOI/exclusivity, not a definitive lease.
- Sandersville deliveries are expected to begin in Q4 2027, so backlog conversion is not near-term operating proof.
- Project cost, funding structure, indebtedness, covenant package, and potential dilution are not yet bounded enough for a buy/add decision.
- Bitcoin mining remains a major current operating and valuation driver until data-center revenue and NOI are delivered.
- No new SA 13F after Q1 2026 was available in the checked sources; the next quarterly filing remains a required recheck.
