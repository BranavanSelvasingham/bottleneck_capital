## What The Evidence Says

The July INTC dislocations are not explained by a found primary-source cancellation, guidance
withdrawal, liquidity default, exchange notice, or official SA exit/reduction.

Positive or thesis-supportive evidence:

- Q1 revenue was $13.6B, up 7% year over year; DCAI revenue was $5.1B, up 22%; Intel Foundry
  revenue was $5.4B, up 16%. Management tied demand to CPUs, wafer offerings, and advanced
  packaging for AI-era workloads.
- Q1 operating cash flow was positive at $1.1B, and the balance sheet included $17.2B of cash
  plus $15.5B of short-term investments against $2.0B of short-term debt and $43.0B of long-term
  debt.
- Intel guided Q2 revenue to $13.8B-$14.8B, GAAP gross margin to 37.5%, non-GAAP gross margin
  to 39.0%, GAAP diluted EPS to $0.08, and non-GAAP diluted EPS to $0.20.
- Intel's June VLSI update said 18A-P entered risk production on the timeline previously shared
  with customers and partners.
- ASML's July 15 release said Intel Foundry is using High-NA EUV on Intel 18A for a subset of
  Core Ultra Series 3 / Panther Lake, with specific layers dual-qualified in Oregon and product
  shipping to customers at yields matched to the NXE platform.
- Intel and Fortinet announced a strategic collaboration to develop Fortinet Security Processor 6,
  using Intel design, packaging, and manufacturing capabilities. This is not enough to prove
  foundry economics, but it is a named external collaboration rather than a pure roadmap claim.

Risk and anti-thesis evidence:

- Q1 GAAP EPS was negative at $(0.73) because restructuring and other charges were large; Intel
  Foundry still had a Q1 segment operating loss of about $2.4B in the Form 10-Q segment table.
- Intel disclosed substantial long-term R&D/manufacturing investment risk, debt/capital-access
  risk, process-technology execution risk, and the possibility of pausing or discontinuing pursuit
  of 14A and other next-generation nodes if sufficient committed demand is not secured.
- Q1 inventory increased sequentially, Q1 PP&E remained very large, and the foundry business still
  primarily supports internal manufacturing. External-customer proof is improving, but revenue
  quality and margins are not yet proven by the Fortinet announcement.
- Intel's own Q2 release says Q2 2026 results are scheduled after the July 23 market close. That
  means the most decision-relevant near-term evidence has not yet arrived during this resolver run.
- The latest local macro/regime events keep energy, shipping, rates, and global-risk pressure high,
  while AI-capex semiconductor evidence remains constructive but crowded.

## Resolver Conclusion

The July 13-17 INTC dislocations are bounded as crowded AI/semicap-beta, pre-earnings, valuation,
foundry-execution, capital-intensity, and macro/geopolitical repricing. They are not, based on the
sources checked, a confirmed primary-source thesis break.

The source gap is only partially resolved:

- Resolved: no found primary-source cancellation, guidance withdrawal, liquidity/default event,
  exchange notice, official SA exit/reduction, or foundry-roadmap break through the checked Intel,
  ASML, SEC, and local event sources.
- Not resolved: Q2 2026 actual results, Q2 cash flow, foundry operating loss trajectory, customer
  economics, forward guidance, 18A/18A-P yield-to-margin conversion, and whether valuation now
  compensates for the turnaround and put-signal risks.
